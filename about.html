About SHAIP

Human-centred AI for the future of medical imaging.

SHAIP Lab is a multidisciplinary medical imaging research lab at the University of Sydney, bringing AI development, human perception, imaging innovation, education and rigorous evaluation into one connected program.

Our identity

The central question at SHAIP Lab is not only what AI can do, but what happens when AI meets a clinician, an image, a learner and a real healthcare environment.

What SHAIP Lab does

We develop and evaluate artificial intelligence across the medical imaging pathway. Our work includes AI for risk, diagnosis and disease characterisation; human–AI interaction and perception; AI-augmented image acquisition and analysis; education and expertise development; and the imaging data and evaluation infrastructure needed to study these systems rigorously.

What connects these areas is our emphasis on human–AI interaction. We combine technical model development with reader studies, eye tracking, clinical evaluation, data science and education research so that AI is assessed in the context in which it will actually be used.

Our approach

SHAIP Lab is designed to cross disciplinary boundaries. A single project may move from data curation and algorithm development to radiologist evaluation, communication, equity analysis and education. This lets us ask a more useful question than whether an algorithm performs well in isolation: does the whole human–AI system work better?

Our mark

The dot of the “i” in SHAIP is an eye. Its four-part iris represents our research programs and places human perception at the centre of the lab’s identity. The mark reflects our view that medical imaging AI should be developed with the person who sees, interprets and acts on its output in mind.

Leadership and mentorship

SHAIP Lab is co-led by Ziba Gandomkar and Seyedamir Tavakoli Taba, with research streams led by Nicola Giannotti, Dania Abu Awwad and Ernest Ekpo. The lab is mentored by Patrick Brennan, supporting our academic, clinical and translational direction.

Our ambition

We aim to produce evidence, methods, datasets and technologies that help medical imaging adopt AI well: technically robust, clinically useful, understandable to people, equitable across populations and supported by an appropriately prepared workforce.

Shape the whole system.

Build better AI. Understand how people use it. Improve imaging. Develop expertise. Evaluate what changes in practice.
