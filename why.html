Why SHAIP?

AI is changing more than algorithms.

It is changing the entire medical imaging ecosystem — from image formation and interpretation to clinical decisions, workforce expertise, communication and education.

Why this lab · Why now

The next challenge in medical imaging AI is not only to make models more accurate. It is to make AI work well with people, imaging systems and healthcare environments.

Medical imaging AI has entered a different phase.

For much of the last decade, the central question was whether an algorithm could detect disease, segment anatomy or predict an outcome. Those questions remain important, but increasingly they are no longer sufficient.

As AI moves into clinical imaging, its effects extend beyond model performance. AI can change what clinicians attend to, when they intervene, how much they trust a finding, how expertise develops, how risk is communicated and how images themselves are generated or processed.

Algorithms meet people

A highly accurate AI system can still fail to improve decisions if its outputs are misunderstood, over-relied upon or poorly integrated into clinical work.

Imaging itself is changing

AI increasingly contributes to reconstruction, enhancement, denoising, segmentation and quantitative imaging — not only interpretation.

Expertise must evolve

Clinicians need new skills to recognise AI strengths, limitations, uncertainty and failure modes while retaining core perceptual expertise.

Benefits must be equitable

Models trained in one population or setting may not be calibrated or generalisable elsewhere. Equity therefore has to be designed and evaluated, not assumed.

Why bring these questions together?

These challenges are often studied separately: algorithm development in one group, perception in another, education elsewhere, and implementation after the fact. SHAIP brings them together because in practice they are deeply connected.

Model→Decision→Perception & expertise→Education→Data & evaluation

A model changes a decision. A decision depends on perception and expertise. Expertise is shaped by education. Education increasingly uses AI. All of these are enabled by the quality of the underlying data and evaluation framework.

Our premise

We believe the most useful medical imaging AI will be created when technical innovation and human-centred evaluation are developed together. That means asking not only “Does the model work?” but also:

  • For whom?
  • Under what conditions?
  • How should it be presented?
  • How does it change human performance?
  • How do we prepare the workforce to use it?

SHAIP exists to shape that future.

Our aim is to create and evaluate AI that improves medical imaging while remaining grounded in human behaviour, clinical context, equity, education and real-world use.
